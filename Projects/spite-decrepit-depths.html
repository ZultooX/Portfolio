Spite: Decrepit Depths
Play as a capable but underestimated spellcasting scholar who will stop at nothing in her pursuit of knowledge, no matter if the undead await in the Decrepit Depths.

For this project my group built our own custom C++ engine with DirectX 11, which came with both pros and cons.

At a glance

  • Team: 18
  • Reference: Diablo 3
  • Duration: 9 weeks at 50% pace (group projects 5 and 6)
  • Engine: Our own C++ / DirectX 11 engine
  • My contributions: collision system, debug drawer, navmesh implementation, animation manager and PBR

Animation manager

To make it easier for my group to add animations, I created a stack-based animation system. Animations are pushed onto the stack and popped when their conditions are met.

For example, an idle animation is pushed first and stays indefinitely. When the player starts walking, a walking animation is pushed on top and overrides the idle. When movement stops, the walking animation is popped, and the idle animation shows again.

This keeps transitions smooth and makes animations simple and flexible to manage. The feedback I got on it from the team was great.

Collision system

We needed to check things like whether projectiles hit their target and whether the player was inside specific regions. I implemented oriented bounding box (OBB) collisions, which are more accurate than axis-aligned boxes for rotated objects.

I used matrix transformations for OBB vs. OBB and OBB vs. sphere checks, with the separating axis theorem (SAT) for precise results. The system ended up being crucial for bullets and event triggers.

PBR

I wanted to learn more about the physics of light and how it interacts with materials, so I worked on physically based rendering. Even early iterations showed key parts of PBR like Fresnel reflections and roughness-based light scattering, and the foundation for realistic lighting was already taking shape.
